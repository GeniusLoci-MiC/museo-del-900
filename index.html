The Casa Rossa of Anacapri, built by John Clay MacKowen between 1887 and 1896, reflects his passion for Capri, narrated in his book Capri (1884). Adjacent to the Torre Aragonese, it combines diverse architectural elements, Islamic-style battlements, and Pompeian red. More than a residence, it was conceived as a house-museum, housing archaeological finds and a decorated courtyard with a majolica staircase connecting the various levels. Today it hosts an art gallery featuring works acquired by the Municipality of Anacapri.

The American John Clay MacKowen (1842–1901) was a physician and a Confederate officer. He interrupted his medical studies to take part in the American Civil War, reaching the rank of lieutenant colonel. After the war, he completed his studies and undertook further training in Europe, in Paris, Vienna, and Munich. Having graduated in 1876, he settled in Italy, first in Rome and then in Capri, where he became involved in a relationship with Mariuccia Cimino, by whom he had a daughter, Giulia Evelina.
